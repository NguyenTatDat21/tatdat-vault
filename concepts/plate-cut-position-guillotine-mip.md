# Plate-and-Cut-Position Guillotine MIP

Biss's 1D "cut and residual element" idea lifted to two dimensions: the model enumerates *plates* (rectangle types reachable by guillotine cuts) and *cut positions*, one variable per (orientation, position, plate) triple.

## Core Structure

- **Plate set $J$** with plate 0 = the panel; plate $j$ has dimensions $(l_j, w_j)$; $\bar J$ = plates whose dimensions equal an item (these double as items); $O=\{h,v\}$.
- **Variables** $x^o_{qj}$ = times plate $j$ is cut at position $q$ with orientation $o$; $y_j$ = plates of type $j$ kept as final items.
- **Coefficient** $a^o_{qkj}=1$ when cutting plate $j$ at position $q$ produces plate $k$ — plate production is a flow conservation over plate types:

$$
\sum_{k\in J}\sum_{o\in O}\sum_{q\in Q(k,o)} a^o_{qkj}x^o_{qk} \;\ge\; \sum_{o}\sum_{q\in Q(j,o)} x^o_{qj} + y_j
$$

so every plate consumed by a cut is supplied by a cut on a larger plate, and kept plates pay out their profit.
- **Position set** is the design choice:
  - **Complete $Q$** — every $q$ reachable as a sum of item sizes (with copy limits $u_i$), generated by a DP extending Martello/Vigo-style position DPs.
  - **Restricted $Q_R$** — cuts only at positions equal to an item size. Theorem 1: a plate fitting at most five items has an optimal solution using only restricted positions; the bound is tight ($z_R/z_U \ge 5/6$).
  - **Symmetric positions** ($q$ and $w_j-q$ both present) are removable and are discarded by the DP.
- **Model reductions**: Cut-Position (the six-smallest-items area test identifies plates with the five-item property, so $Q_R$ suffices) and Redundant-Cut (trim cuts, producing one useful plate, are removed in two structural cases).
- **Variable pricing**: solve the LP relaxation, take plate duals $\pi_j$, and compute reduced profit in linear time:

$$
\tilde p(x^o_{qj}) = \pi_{j_1} + \pi_{j_2} - \pi_j
$$

Keep variables with positive reduced profit, then in the MIP keep those with $\lfloor \tilde p + LP\rfloor > LB$; the LP optimum is the upper bound.
- **Size**: $O(WL(W+L))$ $x$-variables + $n$ $y$-variables — pseudo-polynomial, explosive on instances with many tiny items.
- **Extensions**: covering version (min panels, $y_j\ge d_j$), multi-panel costs, strip-packing with a width variable $z$ and only the first cut horizontal.
- **Pipeline**: plate/position pool by DP → reductions → restricted-position model → LP → pricing → MIP.

The model is the MIP analogue of column generation: the variable pool is huge, and the LP duals decide which columns (plate-and-position cuts) are worth keeping. ^[inferred]

## Why It Matters

- It is the first MIP that covers **all** feasible guillotine cuts, not just stage-restricted, strip-width-restricted, or trim-restricted ones, so the model's optimum is the true guillotine optimum.
- Restricted positions give most of the quality at a fraction of the model: ≤25% of variables and ≤65% of plates for 80% of instances, and the restricted solution was optimal for 48 of the 51 instances solved to optimality.
- Pricing is what makes pseudo-polynomial models practical: the Priced model keeps **under 40% of variables for ~80% of instances**, and 16 of 21 large instances solve to optimality where the direct model solved none.
- The five-item theorem is a usable structural test: it tells you when position restriction is safe, and when it is not (the six-item example).
- Trim-cut elimination (Redundant-Cut) is a general reduction for any plate-based guillotine model. ^[inferred]
- Pseudo-polynomial scale is the ceiling: instances with very small items in large panels (APT-style) are intractable, so the approach is for coarse-grained dimensions. ^[extracted]
- Compared to the only prior guillotine MIP, this formulation is up to two orders of magnitude faster — the plate-and-position representation, not the solver, drives the difference.

## Open Questions

- Is there a position set between $Q_R$ and $Q$ that provably keeps the $5/6$ loss bound below it?
- Can the pricing idea transfer to other pseudo-polynomial 2D models, e.g. the hypergraph/DP state models, so they scale to large panels? ^[inferred]
- What is the true gap of a restricted-position solution on random instances? The paper reports near-optimal empirically, with a worst-case bound only from the constructed example. ^[ambiguous]
