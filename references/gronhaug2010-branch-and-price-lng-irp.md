# A Branch-and-Price Method for a Liquefied Natural Gas Inventory Routing Problem

> [!tldr] Grønhaug, Christiansen, Desaulniers & Desrosiers solve the LNG-IRP introduced in 2009 by branch-and-price: the restricted master problem keeps all inventory and berth constraints, ship-route columns are generated by longest-path subproblems, and three accelerations make it 2–25x faster than a priori path enumeration.

![[_papers/gronhaug2010branchandprice/images/page_2_image_2.jpg]]
*Figure 1 The LNG Supply Chain Highlighting the Parts of the Chain Included in the Study*

## Problem & Novelty

The LNG-IRP inherits three complicating aspects that make standard maritime inventory-routing decompositions fail:

1. a constant rate of the cargo evaporates each day (boil-off, used as fuel during transport);
2. variable production and consumption of LNG;
3. a variable number of tanks unloaded at regasification terminals.

The key novelty: the **inventory constraints stay in the master problem** (the Christiansen 1999 maritime decomposition is rejected) because boil-off synchronizes route duration with delivered quantities — quantity decisions cannot be made inside the subproblem. A time-discrete model is chosen because production/consumption are variables, not fixed rates. ^[inferred]

## Method / Pipeline

- **RMP**: port inventory balance, berth capacity, production/sales bounds, per-ship convexity, integrality of tank counts at delivery ports, and a priori subset of valid inequalities limiting ship visits per time interval (computed from storage capacity, demand bounds, berth capacity, minimum ship capacity, shortest roundtrip; example: 250,000 m³ storage, [12000, 15000] m³/day, berth 1, min ship 120,000 m³ ⇒ at most 3.96 → 3 ships per 15 days).
- **Subproblem**: per-ship longest path in the time-space network with side constraints (full load at pickup, discrete tank unloading per delivery, at most $D^{MX}$ consecutive delivery visits — the *duty* concept).
- **Branching hierarchy**: visits per time interval $a_{ib}^{VI}$ (primary), node removal/forcing, arcs, then number of tanks delivered; fractional branching on the value closest to 1/2, depth-first with backtracking.
- **Accelerations**: (i) relax berth capacity constraints and re-add them as cuts when violated; (ii) a heuristic column generator on an acyclic reduced network assuming full unloading per duty (label-setting; switch to exact DP per ship when it stops improving); (iii) add several columns per iteration by zeroing path reduced cost spread over nodes.

The exact dynamic program keeps labels $E_{kv}=(\bar C^R, T, d_{kv}, \hat Q, \hat L)$ and compares labels only at pickup/destination nodes (dominance by higher reduced cost), because side constraints make labels incomparable at delivery nodes. Quantities are fixed at duty end: all tanks full at pickup, one tank assigned to every delivery node, the highest-node-cost tank takes the remaining cargo, and boil-off $B_v^F$ per period applies only during sailing.

## Key Equations

Reduced cost of a ship-route column (14), with dual prices $\alpha_{it}$ (inventory), $\eta_{it}=\beta_{it}+\sum_b\sigma_{ib}$ (berth, from the valid-inequality duals), $\theta_v$ (convexity):

$$
\bar{C}_{vr}=-C_{vr}+\sum_{i,t}\big(I_i\,Q_{ivtr}\,\alpha_{it}-Z_{ivtr}\,\eta_{it}\big)-\theta_v
$$

Label quantity update at a duty end (Algorithm 3), distributing the boil-off-adjusted cargo $Q-V^{CAP}B^F(T-1)$ over the $\hat L_l$ assigned tanks:

$$
\hat{Q}_l=\frac{\hat{L}_l}{W_v^{MX}}\Big(Q - V^{CAP}B^F(T-1)\Big)
$$

RMP convexity per ship is the same path-flow structure as in [[references/gronhaug2009-supply-chain-optimization-lng]]. ^[inferred]

## Setup & Headline Results

27 instances, groups A–G (1–5 ships, 1–2 tanks per ship, 3–6 ports, 30–75 periods, 257–6,568,845 arcs per ship network), XPRESS 17.1, C++, 3 GHz, 8 GB, 36,000 s limit.

| Metric | Result |
|---|---|
| RMP LP solved | within 1 s for all instances |
| Optimal solutions found (10 h) | 16 of 27 |
| Nodes (max, instance 20) | 709,518; columns > 1M for 7 instances (6,568,845 for instance 20) |
| Time vs path-flow a priori enumeration | 92% reduction on 12 instances, 96% on 6 instances where path-flow hit the limit (instances 3/6/12/14/18/20/21) |
| Berth-capacity cuts generated | 19 rows (instance 6), 7 rows (instance 15), 0 for the rest |
| Instances hitting 36,000 s | 18, 20, 21, 27 |

## Ablations & Limitations

- Cutting-plane generation of the visit limits was dropped in favor of a fixed a priori subset; the valid inequalities closed ≤5% of the integrality gap in tests.
- The heuristic column generator consumes only a fraction of total time; most time is spent in the master problem, not in pricing.
- Optimality was not verified within 10 h for all instances; the method scales to one order of magnitude beyond the 2009 path enumeration but the time limit is still reached on the largest instances.
- Instances 22–27 (75 periods) push enumeration and memory to the limit; a priori enumeration needed 2.5M columns on instance 3 alone.
