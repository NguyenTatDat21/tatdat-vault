# Generative Adversarial Self-Imitation Learning

> [!tldr] Guo, Oh, Singh & Lee (2018) introduce **GASIL**: a generator–discriminator pair where the generator is the agent's own policy and the discriminator imitates the agent's top-K past trajectories. The discriminator becomes a learned internal reward added to the policy gradient, easing credit assignment in sparse and delayed-reward settings.

![[_papers/guo2018generative/images/page_4_chart_12.jpg]]
*Figure 1: Learning curve on 2D point mass. See text for details.*

## Problem & Novelty

Sparse and delayed rewards (reward at episode end only) make credit assignment in RL hard: the environment signal is not informative enough. Existing imitation methods require an external expert policy (BC, GAIL) or prior trajectories (SIL). The novelty is **self-imitation with adversarial training**: no expert is needed, and past good behavior is generated by the agent itself. Contemporaneous work by Gangwani et al. (2018) uses the same method; GASIL is introduced as a unifying framework for GAIL/SIL. ^[inferred]

## Method / Pipeline

1. Maintain a good-episode buffer $B$: keep the top-K episodes by return $R=\sum_t \gamma^t r_t$.
2. Train discriminator $D_\phi(s,a)$ on current-policy trajectories vs. buffer trajectories (JS-divergence form of the GAIL objective):

$$
\max_\phi\ \mathbb{E}_{\tau_\pi}\big[\log D_\phi(s,a)\big]+\mathbb{E}_{\tau_E}\big[\log\big(1-D_\phi(s,a)\big)\big]
$$

3. Use the discriminator as an internal reward. Policy update with advantage estimated from discriminator rewards $Q(s,a)=\mathbb{E}[\log D_\phi \mid s_0=s,a_0=a]$:

$$
\nabla_\theta L=\mathbb{E}_{\tau_\pi}\big[\nabla \log \pi_\theta(a|s)\,Q(s,a)\big]-\lambda\nabla H
$$

4. Combined with PPO via the modified reward $r^\alpha=r-\alpha\log D_\phi(s,a)$:

$$
\nabla J_{PG}-\alpha\nabla\mathcal{L}_{GAIL}=\mathbb{E}\big[\nabla \log \pi_\theta\,\hat{A}^{\alpha}\big]
$$

The paper connects this to optimal reward learning (Singh et al., Sorg et al.): the discriminator is a learned task proxy that guides exploration. ^[inferred]

## Setup & Headline Results

Baselines: PPO, PPO+BC, PPO+SIL, PPO+GASIL. Hyperparameters: FC(64)-FC(64) networks, PPO LR {3e-4,1e-4,5e-5,3e-5}, horizon 2048, 10 epochs, minibatch 64, $\gamma=0.99$, GAE 0.95, discriminator minibatch 128, discriminator updates per batch {1,5,10,20}, discriminator LR {3e-4,1e-4,2e-5,1e-5}, buffer {1000,10000} steps.

| Setting | Outcome |
|---|---|
| 2D point mass (positive objects 5/10, distractors −5, L2 actuation cost) | PPO learns a sub-optimal policy quickly (collects 1 green object); PPO+GASIL learns slower early but finds the better final policy (2 green objects) |
| MuJoCo tasks, reward at episode end | GASIL improves PPO on most tasks, outperforms PPO+BC (buffer has 1K–10K samples) |
| Stochastic Walker2d-v2 (Gaussian noise σ ∈ {0.05, 0.1, 0.5} on observations) | GASIL–PPO gap grows with noise level — robustness to stochastic dynamics |
| Delayed-reward MuJoCo (reward accumulated every 20 steps) | GASIL improves PPO by large margin on all tasks (dense discriminator rewards fill the gap) |
| PPO+SIL comparison | GASIL more sample-efficient and generalizes better under non-stationary buffer |
| Humanoid schedule | α = 0 for first 2M steps, α = 0.02 to 3M steps |

## Ablations & Limitations

- Hyperparameter trade-off: buffers of 500 and 5000 steps both perform poorly — quality vs quantity trade-off; too few and too many discriminator updates per batch also hurt (Figure 7).
- PPO+BC is unstable early (buffer small and noisy); PPO+SIL (advantage-weighted BC with prioritized replay) relies on the buffer quality; GASIL's discriminator generalizes better. ^[inferred]
- Deterministic environments are required to guarantee monotonic policy improvement under the theoretical framing; the stochastic experiments are empirical. ^[inferred]
- Preprint (arXiv, Dec 2018); results on 10-seed MuJoCo averages, no shared-code baselines. ^[ambiguous]
