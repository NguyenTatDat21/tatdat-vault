idx 57 (18,060–18,124, p.722, DOI 53) Erratum to "Modelling Universal Instruction Selection" (Philippsen, Chee, Choffness, Rum, Spenke, Springer 2014, DOI 10.1007/978-3-642-32362-6) — correction to the "saturated sum" C-code example in Fig. 1 of the original paper and to Eq. idx 58 (18,125–18,142, p.723, DOI 54) Finite transducers for structural time-series constraints (Beldiceanu, Carlsson, Douceon, Simonis; TASC/CNRS + Sics + Uppsala) — the finite-transducer approach expresses a constraint as a set of automata over structured data. About 30 catalog constraints were unified with automata, more than 600 new constraints synthesised, and 100,000+ lines generated; the tool is in use and the automata are the structural backbone of a constraint library.

idx 59 (18,143–18,168, p.724, DOI 55) Projection, Consistency, and George Boole: Second-Order Expressions for Propositional Logic (John N. Hooker, CMU) — argues that a constraint-projection algorithm induces a notion of consistency, J-consistency, stronger than path-consistency and incomparable with directed arc-consistency; that a form of second-order logic can be used to define the projection algorithms; and that logic-based Benders is an instance of the projection idea. Full paper in Constraints 21(1).

idx 60 (18,169–18,192, p.725, DOI 56) A Tool for Finding Minimal Independent Support in Boolean Circuits (Ivrii, Malik, Meel, Vardi; KIT + Duke + USC) — minimal independent support (MIS) for a given set of variables in a Boolean circuit is a minimal subset of the support, used for uniform sampling, probabilistic inference, and bounded equivalent reasoning. A circuit is transformed to reduce the length of XORs, then reduced by removing gates; the resulting support is 1/10–1/100 the size of the original support, enabling speedups of 1–2 orders of magnitude, and the support is used as a CNF encoding.

idx 61 (18,193–18,212, pp.726–727, DOI 57) General Game Playing with Stochastic Constraint Programming (Tabary, Cordeau, Muffe, Simonin, Gams; LINA, IRCCyN + University of Ljubljana) — the formalisation of General Game Playing as a stochastic CSP (with chance nodes, uncertainty, and rewards) and the use of CP with Monte-Carlo style sampling. Experiments on three games show that a Monte-Carlo arc-consistency approach combined with UCB (MAC-UCB) outperforms UCT and FC-UCB in 1,350,000 face-offs.

idx 62 (18,213–18,234, p.728, DOI 58) Visual Search Tree Profiling for Modelling and Search Strategies (Shishmarev, Mears, Tack, de la Banda; Monash University, Faculty of IT, Clayton, Australia) — presents a visual profiling tool, the CP profiler, that shows the search tree structure and how it is affected by modelling and search strategies, in particular the interaction between search heuristics and constraints. Available at github.com/cp-profiler.

idx 63 (18,235–18,248, p.729, DOI 59) Long-Haul Fleet Mix and Routing Optimisation with Constraint Programming and Large Neighbourhood Search (Philip Kilby, Tommaso Urli, Optimisation Research Group, NICTA Canberra) — computes efficient midterm fleet configurations for a Queensland-based long-haul trucking carrier on one year's demand data, using a CP model and adaptive LNS to solve the underlying multi-day, multi-commodity, split-delivery, capacitated VRP; a Pareto-based preprocessing step extracts a set of representative days from the full horizon; the solver returns a set of Pareto-equivalent fleet setups trading fleet efficiency against the likelihood of requiring on-hire vehicles and drivers, and can also solve the daily loading and routing problem. Extensive experimental analysis on real-world data; extended version "Fleet Design Optimisation From Historical Data Using Constraint Programming and Large Neighbourhood Search" in Constraints 21(1).

idx 64 (18,249–18,264, p.733, DOI 60) On the Reification of Global Constraints (Beldiceanu, Carlsson, Flener, Pearson; TASC/CNRS Mines Nantes, Sics Kista, Uppsala) — a reification method that works in all CP solvers: a global constraint G(A) is defined as `R(A) ∧ C(A)` with restrictions R on argument bounds, and reified as `R(A) ∧ (C(A) ⇔ b)` for a 0-1 variable b, requiring the negation to satisfy the same R. C(A) can often be reformulated as `F(A,V) ∧ N(A,V)` where F is a total-function constraint uniquely determining V from A and N is a Boolean combination of linear arithmetic (in)equalities over 0-1 variables (reifiable in all known CP solvers), so the reified form is `R(A) ∧ F(A,V) ∧ (N(A,V) ⇔ b)`; example: `alldifferent(⟨v1..vn⟩)` is reified by `sort(⟨v1..vn⟩,⟨w1..wn⟩) ∧ ((w1<w2 ∧ ··· ∧ w_{n-1}<w_n) ⇔ b)`. The determine-and-test scheme reifies 313 of the 381 (82%) constraints of the Global Constraint Catalogue; most uncovered ones are graph constraints involving set variables. Journal: Constraints 18(1):1-6, 2013, DOI 10.1007/s10601-012-9132-0; full details soda.swedish-ict.se/5194.

idx 65 (18,265–18,278, p.734, DOI 61) MDD Propagation for Sequence Constraints (Bergman, Cire, van Hoeve; UConn, Toronto Scarborough, CMU) — establishes MDD consistency on the Sequence constraint is NP-hard, the first negative result of its kind, since Sequence is the global constraint with the most MDD-friendly combinatorial structure (prescribed variable ordering, sub-constraints on contiguous variables, fully handled by bounds reasoning); second, MDD consistency on Sequence is fixed-parameter tractable in the lengths of the sub-sequences (the Among constraints) when the MDD follows the Sequence variable ordering, proved by a constructive generic algorithm that filters one MDD against another; third, a partial MDD propagation algorithm for Sequence based on decomposition into cumulative sums and a new extension of MDD filtering to node-stored information; experiments reduce search-tree size and solving time by orders of magnitude vs domain propagators and vs the MDD decomposition into Among constraints. Journal: JAIR 50:697-722, 2014.

idx 66 (18,279–18,294, p.735, DOI 62) Discrete Optimization with Decision Diagrams (Bergman, Cire, van Hoeve, Hooker) — a novel branch-and-bound for discrete optimization in which relaxed BDDs play the role of the linear-programming relaxation (providing bounds and branching guidance) and restricted BDDs supply a primal heuristic; compilation of relaxed and restricted BDDs from a dynamic-programming model is done by augmenting the DP model with problem-specific relaxation and restriction operators applied to its state-transition graph; the search branches within relaxed BDDs rather than on variable values, which eliminates symmetry during search. Results: competitive with state-of-the-art integer-programming and semidefinite-programming solvers; on the classical MAXCUT benchmark set it identified tighter relaxation bounds than any technique previously, nearly closing the entire optimality gap on four large-scale instances. Journal: INFORMS Journal on Computing, forthcoming 2015.

idx 67 (18,295–18,324, p.736, DOI 63) A Hybrid Approach Combining Local Search and Constraint Programming for a Large Scale Energy Management Problem (Gavranović, Buljubašić; International University of Sarajevo + Ecole des Mines d'Alès LGI2P Nîmes) — from the perspective of a large utility, planning production assets over several years at minimum total operation cost, split into two mutually dependent subproblems: (1) a plant outage schedule satisfying refuelling/maintenance resource limits, (2) an optimal production plan satisfying technical constraints. Heuristic combining constraint satisfaction, local search, and a constructive optimization algorithm; CP with a performant solver was the most reliable and most elegant way to find a feasible outage schedule; artificial constraints added to the model improve diversity and feasibility of the remaining production assignment, and the feasible solution is then improved by a series of local improvements. Close-to-real EDF instances: the method achieves 3 of 15 possible best results. Journal: RAIRO Operations Research 47(4):481-500, 2013, DOI 10.1051/ro/2013053.

idx 68 (18,325–18,342, p.737, DOI 64) Representing and Solving Finite-Domain Constraint Problems using Systems of Polynomials (Jefferson, Jeavons, Green, van Dongen; St Andrews, Oxford, Royal Holloway, UCC) — a system of multivariate polynomials allows a combination of values when the simultaneous assignment makes all polynomials evaluate to zero; polynomials represent finite-domain constraints in a uniform way with continuous constraints, processed by standard computer algebra (Mathematica, REDUCE), unifying CP with other mathematical programming. A Gröbner Basis gives a convenient representation of the whole solution set (correlations between variables, total number of solutions). Computing a Gröbner Basis is doubly exponential in general, but for the systems used for finite-domain constraints it is only singly exponential, comparable with other constraint search techniques; main contributions are a family of polynomial-time algorithms, related to Gröbner Basis construction, that simulate local-consistency algorithms, plus adaptive consistency techniques for polynomial systems. Journal: Annals of Mathematics and AI 67(3-4):359-382, 2013.

idx 69 (18,343–18,360, p.738, DOI 65) A Quadratic Extended Edge-Finding Filtering Algorithm for Cumulative Resource Constraints (Kameugne, Fotso, Scott; Univ. of Maroua, Yaoundé I, Uppsala) — extended edge-finding propagates disjunctive and cumulative resources; complete filtering for cumulative is NP-complete, so cumulative is implemented as a conjunction of polynomial relaxations (timetabling, standard/extended/timetable edge-finding, not-first/not-last, energetic reasoning), whose fixpoints combine to give stronger pruning and smaller trees. Best previously known cumulative extended edge-finding complexity `O(kn^2)` (n tasks, k distinct capacity requirements); this paper proposes an `O(n^2)` algorithm using minimum slack to detect when extended edge-finding may strengthen a domain, more effective on domains already at the edge-finding fixpoint. Experiments on a standard benchmark suite confirm it outperforms previous extended edge-finding algorithms; edge-finding + extended edge-finding is slightly worse than edge-finding alone in most cases, but on some instances the stronger combined pruning gives substantial gains, and the method is competitive with the state-of-the-art edge-finding algorithms. Journal: International Journal of Planning and Scheduling 1(4):264-284, 2013.

idx 70 (18,361–18,378, p.740, DOI 66) Achieving Domain Consistency and Counting Solutions for Dispersion Constraints (Gilles Pesant; Polytechnique de Montréal + CIRRELT, Université de Montréal) — balance/fairness is required in assembly line balancing, rostering (weekends off, night shifts), and the Balanced Academic Curriculum Problem. spread and deviation express balance by constraining the mean and the overall deviation from the mean, and the only known practical filters achieve bounds consistency. dispersion is a more general constraint including spread and deviation as special cases and other variants, with an efficient **domain consistency** algorithm, extended to count solutions so it can be used in counting-based search (a generic family of branching heuristics that frees the user from writing problem-specific heuristics). Empirical evidence of practical improvement: state-of-the-art results on the Nurse to Patient Assignment Problem and the Balanced Academic Curriculum Problem. Journal: INFORMS Journal on Computing, forthcoming; also CIRRELT-2015-08.

idx 71 (18,379–18,390, p.741, DOI 67) meSAT: Multiple Encodings of CSP to SAT (Stojadinović, Marić; Faculty of Mathematics, University of Belgrade) — a system translating finite linear CSP specifications into SAT using several well-known encodings (direct, log, support, order, compact-order), their combinations, and SAT encodings of cardinality constraints. Efficiency of direct, support, and order encodings improves through the combinations direct-support and direct-order; efficient encodings of global constraints within direct encoding and a correctness proof of a variant of order encoding are given. A methodology selects a suitable encoding from simple syntactic features of the instance, modifying ArgoSmArT-kNN (k-nearest neighbours, originally for SAT); evaluated on large public corpora the selection improves over existing encodings and state-of-the-art tools, and if syntactically similar instance families exist the selection can be trained on very easy instances, greatly reducing training time, on the observation that a solver efficient on easier instances of a problem is often efficient on harder instances of the same problem. Journal: Constraints 19(4):380-403, 2014; Serbian Ministry of Science grant 174021.

idx 72 (18,391–18,404, p.742, DOI 68) Constraint programming for LNG ship scheduling and inventory management (Willem-Jan van Hoeve, Tepper School of Business, CMU) — designing delivery schedules (annual delivery schedules of LNG loadings, deliveries, and ship movements) from liquefaction to regasification terminals, combining ship routing with inventory management at the terminals; used for supply-chain design analysis of new LNG projects (ships, production-side terminals, berths, storage tanks). Goel et al. (Journal of Heuristics, 2012) introduced the LNG inventory-routing MIP model and MIP heuristic, both with scalability issues. Two CP models based on a disjunctive scheduling representation: ship visits to facilities are optional activities, distances are sequence-dependent setup times for the disjunctive resource representing each ship's schedule, and inventory at each location is a cumulative resource; the CP models are much smaller and more scalable than the corresponding MIP models, and an iterative search heuristic generates good feasible solutions; on large-scale test instances it finds better solutions than MIP-based approaches while being 4-10× faster on average. Journal: European Journal of Operational Research 241(3):662-673, 2015 (Goel, Slusky, van Hoeve, Furman, Shao).

idx 73 (18,405–18,414, volume pp.743-744; Author Index runs pp.745-755 as a separate book feature) Revisiting the Limits of MAP Inference by MWSS on Perfect Graphs (Adrian Weller, University of Cambridge) — Boolean binary weighted CSPs without hard constraints, maximising `f(x) = Σ_i ψ_i(x_i) + Σ_{(i,j)∈E} ψ_ij(x_i,x_j)` (MAP/MPE inference, the configuration of maximum probability). A [2] showed this problem (discrete models of any arity) reduces to a maximum weight stable set (MWSS) in a derived weighted graph called an NMRF (closely related to the microstructure complement of the dual representation), introducing node pruning; different reparameterizations lead to different pruned NMRFs, and a perfect pruned NMRF solves the initial problem in polynomial time. This paper extends [5] to all possible reparameterizations of Boolean binary models, giving a simple exact characterization of the enlarged tractable set, and shows how they can be identified efficiently, settling the power of the approach on this class.
